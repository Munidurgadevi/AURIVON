Projector or big-screen TV: which suits your room

Light, room size and seating distance decide the answer. If your room can be darkened and your seats are more than three metres from the screen, a projector gives the most cinematic image for the money. In bright, multi-purpose living rooms, a large TV stays punchy at any hour and needs no screen or blackout curtains. We visit, measure the room and its light, and recommend the option that suits how your family really watches.

Ambient light is the biggest deciding factor. A projector that looks stunning at night can look washed out with daylight pouring through the windows, while a TV makes its own light and has no such problem. If most of your movie nights happen after dark, blackout curtains and a projector are an easy win.

Think about everyday use as well. Cricket in the afternoon, the news in the morning and cartoons after school all favour a TV, which is ready the moment you switch it on. A projector rewards planned, immersive viewing: films, series and big matches with friends, with the lights down.

Whichever you choose, sound matters as much as the picture. A large screen with weak sound feels flat, so we plan the speakers together with the screen from the very first visit. This ensures balanced audio, clear dialogue and an immersive cinematic experience tailored to your room. We also consider room size, seating position and speaker placement for optimal performance. Every detail is carefully planned to deliver consistent, high-quality sound throughout your entertainment space.

A projector is a cinema for the evening. A TV is a window for the whole day.

The AURIVON team

Four questions to ask before you decide

  1. Can the room be made dark? Curtains or blinds that block daylight are what let a projector shine.
  2. How far will you sit from the screen? Beyond three metres, a projector image feels far more like a cinema.
  3. How often do you watch in daylight? If it is most of the time, a bright TV is the safer choice.
  4. Do you want the screen to disappear? A projector screen can roll away, while a TV is always on show.

How we hide the wires without opening your walls

Cable channels and clever routing keep wires out of sight, even in rooms that are already finished. We use slim paintable channels, skirting-board routes and existing ceiling voids to carry speaker and HDMI cables without breaking a single wall. Every run is labelled and left with a little spare length, so future upgrades stay easy. The result is a clean room with no dust, no repainting and no visible cables.

Planning is what makes this possible. Before touching a single cable, we walk the room with you and mark the screen, speaker and seating positions, then trace the shortest hidden route between them. Different cable types are kept apart, with power on one side and signal on the other, so you never hear hum or lose picture quality.

Where a channel is unavoidable, we choose a colour-matched or paintable finish so it blends into the wall, the skirting or the ceiling edge. For wall-mounted speakers we use slim, flat cabling that sits neatly behind trim. Everything is labelled at both ends, which makes future changes quick and tidy.

The work is also clean. We protect floors and furniture, use dust-catching tools while drilling is needed, and leave the room ready to use the same day. Our team carefully cleans the installation area before leaving. We ensure every component is neatly installed and properly tested.

The best installation is the one you never notice.

The AURIVON team

What we use to keep your walls untouched

  1. Paintable cable channels along corners and edges, finished to match your wall colour.
  2. Skirting-board and trim routes for low-level runs that stay completely out of view.
  3. Existing ceiling voids and false-ceiling spaces to carry cables across the room.
  4. Flat, slim cables for short surface runs behind wall-mounted speakers and screens.

Care 10 Sep 2026 4 min read

Five habits that keep your system sounding new

Placement, ventilation, dusting and updates are small routines that protect your equipment for years. Give the receiver breathing room, dust speaker grilles with a soft brush and avoid stacking devices on top of each other. Keep firmware updated, and switch the system off during long power cuts or storms. A yearly check by our team keeps the sound balanced and the picture sharp.

Heat is the quiet enemy of electronics. Amplifiers and receivers work hard, and when warm air has nowhere to go, components age faster and the sound can become harsh. Good airflow costs nothing and is the single easiest way to add years to your system.

Habits matter more than gadgets. A few minutes each month is enough to keep everything clean, cool and up to date, and it saves you from expensive repairs later. Our team also checks the connections and room balance on a yearly visit.

Finally, think about how loudly the system is played. Running music or films near the top of the volume range stresses speakers and amplifiers and shortens their life. Keep the level comfortable, use a surge protector at the wall, and tell us if you ever notice crackling, hum or a sudden drop in sound. Regular maintenance helps keep your system performing at its best. Our team can inspect the equipment, identify potential issues early and recommend the right solutions. Proper care ensures reliable performance and a better listening experience for years to come.

Five minutes a month saves years of wear.

The AURIVON team

The five habits

  1. Give it room to breathe. Leave a hand's width of free space around the receiver and amplifier.
  2. Dust gently. Use a soft, dry brush on grilles and vents, and never spray liquid on a speaker.
  3. Keep it updated. Install firmware and app updates so your system stays stable and secure.
  4. Avoid stacking. Place devices side by side, not on top of each other, so heat can escape.
  5. Switch off in storms. Turn the system off at the wall during long power cuts and thunderstorms.
